\begin{abstract}
We formalize the Bombieri--Vinogradov theorem in Lean 4 in the
level-of-distribution form used by modern bounded-prime-gap arguments.
For every real $\theta<1/2$ and $A\geq1$, one constant controls, for every
real $x\geq3$, the sum over $q\leq\lfloor x^\theta\rfloor$ of the largest
reduced-residue-class discrepancy
$|\pi(x;q,a)-\pi(x)/\varphi(q)|$ by $x/(\log x)^A$.
The proof develops a character large sieve, a corrected finite Vaughan
identity, a Vaughan mean-value theorem, a character-form Siegel--Walfisz
estimate, a globally centered weighted theorem, and an equality-safe
partial-summation conversion to prime counts. The final declaration is
kernel checked without project-local axioms or numerical certificates.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}

The Bombieri--Vinogradov theorem replaces a pointwise distribution estimate
for primes in arithmetic progressions by an average over moduli. It supplies
level of distribution $1/2$ in the usual strict sense: every fixed exponent
below $1/2$ is allowed, but the endpoint itself is not asserted. This average
distribution theorem is a central analytic input to sieve methods and, in
particular, to Maynard's bounded-gap framework \cite{Maynard2015}.

The present development proves the analytic theorem itself. Its public
statement is matched to the hypothesis consumed by the pinned
\texttt{PrimeGapsLib} development \cite{PrimeGapsLib}. We do not formalize
the bounded-gap consequence here, and we do not assert Elliott--Halberstam or
any distribution exponent at least $1/2$.

\section{Exact statement and conventions}

For a real cutoff $x$, let $\pi(x)$ denote the number of primes $p\leq x$.
For $q\in\mathbb N$ and $a\in(\mathbb Z/q\mathbb Z)^\times$, let
$\pi(x;q,a)$ count primes $p\leq x$ whose image modulo $q$ is $a$.
All cutoff inequalities are inclusive. Euler's totient is denoted
$\varphi(q)$.

\begin{theorem}[Bombieri--Vinogradov]\label{thm:bv}
For every $\theta\in\mathbb R$ with $\theta<\tfrac12$ and every
$A\in\mathbb R$ with $A\geq1$, there is a real $c>0$ such that, for every
real $x\geq3$,
\[
  \sum_{1\leq q\leq\lfloor x^\theta\rfloor}
  \max_{a\in(\mathbb Z/q\mathbb Z)^\times}
  \left|\pi(x;q,a)-\frac{\pi(x)}{\varphi(q)}\right|
  \leq \frac{c x}{(\log x)^A}.
\]
\end{theorem}

In Lean the maximum is an indexed supremum over the finite type of units.
When $\lfloor x^\theta\rfloor=0$, the modulus sum is empty. The definition
therefore also handles negative $\theta$ without an auxiliary case in the
public statement.

The public theorem is
\leanname{BombieriVinogradov.bombieriVinogradov} in
\texttt{Solution.lean}. Its type is the root proposition
\texttt{BombieriVinogradov}, whose exact average form is connected to
\leanname{BombieriVinogradov.averagePrimeDiscrepancy} by reflexivity.

\section{Sources and fidelity}

The theorem originates in independent work of Bombieri \cite{Bombieri1965}
and A.~I. Vinogradov \cite{Vinogradov1965}. The exact quantifier surface used
here follows equation~(1.3) of Maynard \cite{Maynard2015}, with endpoints and
definitions aligned to \texttt{PrimeGapsLib} commit
\path{1faa7b14e82ddebc2772dfb9153922f01b106477}. Vaughan's lecture notes
\cite{Vaughan2025} are the primary working exposition for the classical
large-sieve proof, with Harper's notes \cite{Harper2015} as background.

The development is a reconstruction rather than a line-by-line transcription.
One displayed finite Vaughan identity in the working exposition had a sign
and cutoff mismatch with its coefficient algebra. Before formalization, the
identity was expanded on squarefree composite and unequal-cutoff cases. The
formal theorem uses the convention established by the coefficient identity
and retains the source positivity hypotheses even where the final algebraic
equality does not use them. This is recorded as an adaptation, not silently
presented as a verbatim formalization.

\section{Proof architecture}

The repository follows an inward-to-outward dependency order:
definitions, reusable helpers, independent proof branches, assembly modules,
and finally the public boundary. The mathematical route has six stages.

\subsection{The additive and character large sieves}

The additive large sieve is proved for separated Farey samples. The proof
isolates the spacing estimate, Fejer majorization, Schur-type operator bound,
duality, and the final smooth-majorant argument in separate modules. It is
then transferred to primitive Dirichlet characters using Gauss-sum inversion
and Parseval on the unit group.

For an integer interval $M<n\leq M+N$, the resulting character estimate has
the explicit form
\[
 \sum_{1\leq q\leq Q}\frac q{\varphi(q)}
 \sum_{\chi\ ({\rm mod}\ q)}^{*}
 \left|\sum_{M<n\leq M+N}a_n\chi(n)\right|^2
 \leq 36(N+Q^2)\sum_{M<n\leq M+N}|a_n|^2.
\]
The constant $36$ is the exact witness exported by the checked declaration
\begin{center}
\leanname{BombieriVinogradov.LargeSieve.characterLargeSieve}.
\end{center}

\subsection{Vaughan's identity}

The finite Vaughan identity is proved first as an equality of arithmetic
functions and then applied coefficientwise to arbitrary complex test
functions. Splitting the divisor ranges yields two Type~I contributions, a
Type~II contribution, and the short initial Mangoldt sum. The formal owner is
\leanname{BombieriVinogradov.VaughanIdentity.vaughanIdentity}; its theorem
comment explicitly records the corrected sign and cutoff convention.

\subsection{The Vaughan mean-value estimate}

The large sieve is applied to the Vaughan decomposition branch by branch.
Rather than hiding the endpoint conversion in coercion simplification, the
natural-cutoff theorem and the comparison with the real majorant are separate
lemmas. The final theorem
\leanname{BombieriVinogradov.VaughanMeanValue.vaughanMeanValue} uses the
explicit coefficient $200000$. This coefficient is intentionally coarse but
fully checked and uniform over the stated parameters.

\subsection{Siegel--Walfisz}

The small-conductor range requires a character-form Siegel--Walfisz theorem.
The development proves a zero-free alternative, treats the possible real
quadratic exceptional zero, derives the needed explicit formula, and splits
large and bounded heights. Two pinned dependencies provide reused analytic
infrastructure: \texttt{Robin1984} supplies Riemann-xi divisor and
logarithmic-derivative identities, while \texttt{PrimeNumberTheoremAnd}
supplies prime-counting definitions and related analytic foundations.

\begin{samepage}
The exported theorem
\begin{center}
\leanname{BombieriVinogradov.SiegelWalfisz.siegel_walfisz}
\end{center}
states that there is one absolute rate $a>0$ such that, for every logarithmic
modulus exponent
$B>0$, a coefficient $C_B>0$ uniformly bounds every nonprincipal character
sum by
\[
  C_B x\exp\!\left(-a\sqrt{\log x}\right)
\]
whenever the modulus is at most $(\log x)^B$. The rate is outside the
quantifier over $B$; this quantifier order is essential in the later split.
\end{samepage}

\subsection{The centered weighted theorem}

Character orthogonality converts the progression discrepancy into character
sums. The proof separates small and large conductors, uses the
Siegel--Walfisz theorem in the former range and the Vaughan mean-value theorem
in the latter, and keeps the conductor weights required by the source
estimate. Logarithmic powers and square-root terms are absorbed only after a
strict positive gap below $1/2$ has been chosen.

The resulting declaration below proves, for all real $X\geq3$,
\begin{center}
\leanname{BombieriVinogradov.WeightedBombieriVinogradov.}\\
\leanname{weighted_bombieri_vinogradov}.
\end{center}
\[
 \sum_{1\leq q\leq\lfloor X^\theta\rfloor}
 \max_{a}\max_{y\leq\lfloor X\rfloor}
 \left|\psi(y;q,a)-\frac{\psi(y)}{\varphi(q)}\right|
 \ll_{\theta,A}\frac X{(\log X)^A}.
\]
The center is the global finite Mangoldt sum $\psi(y)/\varphi(q)$, exactly
the convention required by the later prime-counting consumer.

\subsection{Prime powers and reciprocal-log Abel summation}

For natural $N$, define the progression and global prime-log sums
$\vartheta(N;q,a)$ and $\vartheta(N)$. The formalization proves
\[
 0\leq\psi(N)-\vartheta(N)\leq2\sqrt N\log N
\]
and the analogous progression error by comparison with the global error.
After centering, every theta-prefix is therefore controlled by the weighted
maximum plus $4\sqrt X\log X$.

Let $F(n)$ be the centered theta coefficient. Since $F(1)=0$, equality-safe
finite Abel summation from $2$ to $N$ gives
\[
 \left|\sum_{2\leq n\leq N}\frac{F(n)}{\log n}\right|
 \leq \frac{D}{\log2}
\]
whenever every cumulative sum through $N$ has absolute value at most $D$.
The proof explicitly covers the equal-endpoint and empty-interval cases; no
strict cutoff is smuggled into the theorem.

Division by $\log p$ turns the prime-log weights into the exact inclusive
prime count. Summing the prime-power error over moduli contributes
\[
 \lfloor X^\theta\rfloor\,4\sqrt X\log X.
\]
With $\delta=1/2-\theta>0$, the eventual inequality
$(\log X)^{A+1}\leq X^\delta$ absorbs this term into
$4X/(\log X)^A$. A symbolic bounded-interval rescaling extends the estimate
to every $X\geq3$; no finite numerical verification is used.

\section{Verification and trust boundary}

The project is pinned to Lean~4.33.1 and Mathlib~4.33.1. The final project
declaration uses only \texttt{propext}, \texttt{Classical.choice}, and
\texttt{Quot.sound}. The deliberate hole in \texttt{Challenge.lean} is the
Palomar problem statement and is excluded from the project sorry count;
\texttt{Solution.lean} contains no placeholder.

Challenge imports only Mathlib and defines its statement vocabulary directly.
Solution imports the project proof. Both disable automatic implicit
parameters. A boundary audit separately elaborates all configured definition
and theorem pairs, and the pinned Comparator checks their exported forms.
NanoDa and Lean's default kernel provide independent replays of the Solution
artifact. These checks establish formal derivability and statement matching;
they do not establish historical novelty, source fidelity, or expert review
on their own.

No generated finite certificate is part of the proof. Automation was used for
source search, proof design, focused Lean probes, routine algebra, refactoring,
and verification. The public Lean terms, rather than the agent transcript or
private research records, are the proof object presented for checking.

\section{Limitations and review status}

The formal theorem stops strictly below distribution exponent $1/2$ and does
not imply Elliott--Halberstam. The project does not formalize the downstream
bounded-gap theorem. The source statement, endpoints, centered convention,
and dependency assumptions have been self-audited and mechanically checked,
but the work has not yet received independent expert mathematical review.
